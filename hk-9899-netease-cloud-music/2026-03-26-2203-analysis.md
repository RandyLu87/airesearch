## Bottom line

NetEase Cloud Music is no longer a turnaround story in distress. It has become a profitable, cash-rich online music platform with a much healthier revenue mix: online music services grew 12.0% in 2025 and membership subscription revenue grew to RMB5.05 billion, while the lower-quality social entertainment business kept shrinking. That said, at a verified close of HK$126.6 on 2026-03-26 and a market cap of HK$27.84 billion, the stock is not obviously cheap after adjusting for earnings quality. The reported 2025 profit benefited from a large deferred tax credit, and consolidated revenue still fell 2.4% for the year. My stance is `watchlist`, not an aggressive buy, unless price gets cheaper or the core music business proves it can re-accelerate revenue without giving back margins.

## Change since last note

The prior root-level note correctly identified that profitability had structurally improved, but the fresh conclusion is slightly less bullish. The business is stronger financially than before, yet the revenue side has not fully recovered. FY2025 revenue was still negative year-on-year, and headline net profit was flattered by a one-off deferred tax credit. On the positive side, balance-sheet strength improved further and management has at least started small buybacks.

## Business quality

The business now rests mainly on online music rather than social entertainment. In 2025, online music services generated RMB5.99 billion, up 12.0%, while social entertainment services and others fell 32.0% to RMB1.77 billion. Membership subscription revenue rose from RMB4.46 billion to RMB5.05 billion, showing that paid music is becoming the economic core of the platform.

This is a real business with user value, brand recognition, and a differentiated community product in Chinese music. But the moat is not as deep as the market’s best internet platforms. Switching costs are moderate, content licensing still matters, and competition for user time comes not just from Tencent Music but also from short video, audio platforms, and broader entertainment apps.

Governance deserves a Hong Kong/China discount. The company is controlled within the NetEase group and relies on contractual arrangements for restricted PRC businesses. That is normal for Chinese internet listings, but it remains a real legal and minority-shareholder risk rather than a footnote. Another non-trivial point: one external customer accounted for RMB1.06 billion of 2025 revenue, or roughly 13.7%, so there is some customer concentration.

## Financial quality

FY2025 revenue was RMB7.76 billion, down 2.4%, but gross profit still rose 3.3% to RMB2.77 billion and gross margin expanded to 35.7% from 33.7%. Operating profit reached RMB1.62 billion, up 38.5%, and adjusted operating profit was RMB1.73 billion, up 32.4%. This shows the business is no longer subsidizing itself to buy growth.

The quality caveat is below the net-profit line. Reported profit for 2025 was RMB2.75 billion, but this included an income tax credit of RMB678.1 million, mainly from a RMB746.7 million deferred tax asset recognition, partly offset by RMB61.0 million of Pillar Two top-up tax. That makes the plain trailing P/E look cheaper than the underlying operating reality.

The balance sheet is very strong. As of 2025-12-31, the company had RMB9.06 billion of current bank deposits, RMB1.40 billion of non-current bank deposits, RMB3.12 billion of cash and cash equivalents, and almost no financial leverage disclosed. Total equity was RMB12.87 billion against total assets of RMB16.46 billion. No assets were pledged.

Capital allocation is mixed rather than clearly shareholder-friendly. The board still proposed no dividend for 2025. More notably, the company disclosed that the approximately RMB2.584 billion equivalent of IPO net proceeds remained unused as of 2025-12-31 and was still sitting in short-term bank deposits. Buybacks started in February 2026, but they were small: by 2026-02-20 the company had repurchased 371,350 shares under the mandate, only 0.1708% of issued shares.

Cash conversion is harder to underwrite for 2025 right now because the annual results announcement did not yet include the full audited cash flow statement. The latest audited annual report shows 2024 operating cash flow of RMB1.77 billion. As of 2026-03-26, I would wait for the 2025 annual report before treating 2025 free cash flow as fully confirmed.

## Valuation

The latest verified market data I found is from 2026-03-26: share price HK$126.6, market cap HK$27.84 billion, enterprise value HK$14.30 billion, and trailing P/E 9.05x. On first look that appears inexpensive.

But the headline multiple needs adjustment. If I strip out the RMB746.7 million deferred tax credit from 2025 adjusted net profit, normalized earnings are closer to RMB2.11 billion. On that basis, the stock is trading around 12x normalized earnings. On an EV basis, the stock is at roughly 7.5x 2025 adjusted operating profit, which is not demanding, but it is also not a deep-value mispricing for a low-growth China internet asset with no dividend and real governance complexity.

My fair-value frame is 11x-13x normalized earnings for the current business quality and growth profile. That implies an equity value of about HK$25.3 billion to HK$29.9 billion. The bull case, where online music keeps compounding double digits and social entertainment stops shrinking materially, could justify roughly 14x normalized earnings, or about HK$32.2 billion. The bear case, where core growth slows and cost discipline loosens, could justify only 9x-10x, or roughly HK$20.7 billion to HK$23.0 billion.

That leaves the current market cap near the middle of a reasonable valuation range, not near the bottom. In other words, the stock is not expensive, but the margin of safety is thin.

## Risks

1. Online music subscription growth slows to low single digits, leaving the company with weak consolidated revenue growth again.
2. Content licensing fees or revenue-sharing costs re-accelerate and reverse margin gains.
3. Social entertainment declines faster or for longer than management expects, offsetting progress in the core music business.
4. VIE and PRC regulatory risks remain real, and NetEase group control limits minority shareholders’ influence.
5. Capital allocation stays conservative but inefficient: no dividend, large idle cash balances, and buybacks too small to matter.

## What would change my mind

I would get more constructive if two things happen together: first, consolidated revenue returns to clear high-single-digit growth for at least two reporting periods; second, that growth comes without a sharp deterioration in gross margin or marketing efficiency. A clearer shareholder-return policy would also help a lot, especially a meaningful buyback pace or a standing dividend.

I would get more negative if online music revenue growth decelerates sharply, if content costs begin rising faster than revenue, or if the large cash balance is used for low-return expansion instead of being returned to shareholders or at least deployed into clearly accretive uses.

## Action

`Watchlist`.

For new money, I do not see enough margin of safety at roughly HK$126-127. My preferred buy zone would be below about HK$110, or alternatively at a similar price only if later results show the core online music business sustaining stronger growth than the current evidence supports. If you already hold it, the case is closer to a patient hold than an add, assuming the position size is modest.
